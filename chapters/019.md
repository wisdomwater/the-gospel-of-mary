# Day 19 — Where Are You Going?

> *“They questioned the soul, ‘Where do you come from, murderer? And where are you going, wanderer?’”*
>
> *“The soul answered and said, ‘What binds me has been slain, and what surrounds me has been overcome.’”*

The accusation is severe. The powers do not simply ask the soul where it is going; they give it names. *Murderer. Wanderer.* Before the soul can describe itself, something else attempts to provide the definition. The question is no longer merely whether the soul is free, but whether it will accept the identity assigned to it.

We know something of this experience. Names have a way of attaching themselves to us. Some come from other people, some from cultures and communities, and some arise from our own inner voice. Failure. Difficult. Weak. Unworthy. Too much. Not enough. Sometimes a name begins with something real—we failed at something, hurt someone, lost our way—and then quietly expands until one event becomes an identity. *I failed* becomes *I am a failure.*

The soul refuses that movement. Interestingly, it does not defend itself by presenting a better list of accomplishments. It does not argue, “No, here is why I am good.” Instead, it speaks about liberation: “What binds me has been slain, and what surrounds me has been overcome.” Its identity is no longer determined by the powers that once enclosed it.

There is an important difference between responsibility and identity. We can take responsibility for what we have done without believing that our actions exhaust the mystery of who we are. We can make amends, change direction, and learn from consequences without carrying an old name forever. Repentance itself assumes that a human being is more than the thing being repented of.

And perhaps we are all wanderers in some sense. We rarely travel toward wholeness in a straight line. We lose our way, recognize something, begin again, and sometimes discover that even our detours have taught us how to see. The powers use “wanderer” as an accusation, but wandering need not mean we are lost forever.

The question remains: “Where are you going?” The soul's answer is ultimately not a destination but a freedom. What once defined and enclosed me no longer has the final word. I am still moving.

**Prayer**

> God who knows my true name, free me from the names that have become prisons. Give me courage to take responsibility for what I have done without reducing myself to my failures. When old voices tell me who I must always be, remind me that transformation is possible. Loosen what binds me, open what surrounds me, and lead me onward in freedom. Amen.
