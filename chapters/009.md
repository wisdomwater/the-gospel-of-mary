## Day 9 — Grace Will Shelter You

> *“Then Mary stood up. She greeted all of them and said to her brothers, ‘Do not weep and do not grieve or be irresolute, for his grace will be with you all and will shelter you.’”*
>
> *“Rather, let us praise his greatness, for he prepared us and made us truly human.”*

The disciples are afraid, and Mary stands up. It is a small detail, but an important one. She does not remain absorbed in her own grief, nor does she wait for someone else to restore courage to the room. She rises in the middle of their fear and reminds them of something they have temporarily forgotten: grace is still with them.

Mary does not promise that nothing painful will happen. She has heard the same question the others have asked: if their teacher was not spared, why should they expect to be? Her answer is not an assurance of safety but an assurance of presence. “His grace will be with you all and will shelter you.” Whatever lies ahead, they will not meet it alone.

Perhaps this is one of the quieter distinctions between faith and certainty. Certainty wants to know that events will unfold according to our hopes. Faith discovers that we can move forward even when we do not know. Grace does not always remove the difficult road in front of us; sometimes grace is what allows us to walk it without being consumed by fear.

Then Mary says something beautiful: “He prepared us and made us truly human.” Spiritual transformation is sometimes imagined as an escape from our humanity, as though enlightenment means rising above weakness, emotion, embodiment, and limitation. Mary points in another direction. The work of the Savior makes us more fully human. Perhaps becoming truly human means becoming capable of living from something deeper than fear—capable of compassion, courage, presence, and love even while remaining vulnerable.

There may be moments when we need someone like Mary to stand up beside us and remind us of this. There may also be moments when we are called to stand up for someone else. We do not need perfect words or answers. Sometimes we simply remind one another that grace has not disappeared, that fear does not have the final word, and that we have already been given something with which to meet what comes next.

**Prayer**

> Grace that shelters me, remain near when fear makes me forget what I have already received. Help me trust your presence without demanding certainty about what comes next. Make me more fully human—more compassionate, more courageous, more present, and more free to love. And when those around me lose heart, give me the gentleness to help them remember that grace is still here. Amen.
