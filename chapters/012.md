# Day 12 — The Eye Between

> *He answered and said to me, “Blessed are you, that you did not waver at the sight of me. For where the mind is, there is the treasure.”*
>
> *I said to him, “Lord, does the one who sees a vision see it through the soul or through the spirit?”*

*The Savior answered, “Neither through the soul nor through the spirit, but the mind which is between the two is what sees the vision.”*

Mary wants to understand what has happened to her. She has seen the Lord in a vision, but through what faculty did she see? Was this an experience of the soul, or of the spirit? The Savior's answer refuses the choice. There is something between them—the *mind*, often represented by the Greek word *nous*—and this is what sees.

For ancient spiritual writers, *nous* could mean more than our ordinary stream of thoughts. It could point toward an inner faculty of perception, the part of us capable of recognizing what cannot simply be grasped by the senses. We might think of it as an inner eye: not opposed to reason, but deeper than the constant analysis, categorizing, remembering, and anticipating that usually occupies our attention.

We may have glimpsed this kind of perception ourselves. There are moments when we suddenly understand something without being able to trace every step that brought us there. A truth we have heard many times unexpectedly becomes real. We look at someone we love and, for an instant, see them without all the stories and expectations we normally place upon them. We become quiet in prayer and recognize that the silence is not empty. Nothing new has necessarily appeared; something in us has become able to see.

This may be why the Savior connects mind and treasure. “Where the mind is, there is the treasure.” Our attention gradually reveals what we value, but it also shapes what we become capable of perceiving. A mind continually scattered among fear, resentment, comparison, and craving may struggle to notice the quieter movements of grace. Contemplation does not create the sacred; it teaches the inner eye to become receptive to what is already present.

Perhaps we do not need extraordinary visions. Mary's question can become much simpler for us: What am I learning to see? Beneath the noise of my thoughts and the urgency of my desires, is there a quieter way of knowing waiting to awaken? The treasure may already be here. The invitation is to become still enough to perceive it.

**Prayer**

> God of light, quiet the noise that keeps me from seeing clearly. Gather my scattered attention and awaken the deeper eye within me. Help me notice what fear, hurry, and craving cause me to overlook, and teach me to recognize your presence in what is already here. Where my attention goes, let my treasure increasingly be found in love. Amen.
