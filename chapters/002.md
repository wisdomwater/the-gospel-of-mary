# Day 2 — When We Lose Our Way

> *Peter said to him, “Since you have explained everything to us, tell us this also: What is the sin of the world?”*
>
> *The Savior said, “There is no sin, but you create sin when you mingle as in adultery, which is called sin.”*

Peter asks a question human beings have been asking for a very long time. What is wrong with us? Why do we hurt one another, betray what we love, and sometimes act against the very things we know to be good? We might expect Jesus to answer by giving a definition of sin or perhaps a list of things that offend God. Instead, the Gospel of Mary gives us a stranger and more provocative response: “There is no sin.”

This does not seem to mean that nothing we do matters. The very next words acknowledge that we create sin through the way we live. But sin is not described as some substance woven into the fabric of creation, nor as a permanent stain upon our deepest identity. It arises when things become mixed together that do not belong together, when desire, fear, attachment, and confusion pull us away from the truth of who we are.

Perhaps we know this experience more intimately than we realize. There are moments when we act from a place that feels almost foreign to us. Anger speaks before compassion has a chance to answer. Fear makes us protect ourselves at someone else's expense. Desire promises satisfaction if we will only obey it. Later, when the urgency has passed, we sometimes look back and wonder why we behaved in a way so contrary to the person we want to be.

Mary offers another way of seeing these moments. Our failures may be real without being our deepest identity. We can take responsibility for the harm we cause without believing that harm reveals the final truth about us. Something has become confused, tangled, or divided within us, and the work of transformation is not merely to condemn what went wrong but to become whole again.

That changes the way repentance can feel. Instead of standing before God convinced that we are fundamentally corrupt, perhaps repentance is the gentle work of becoming rightly ordered again. We notice where fear has become entangled with love, where craving has disguised itself as need, or where woundedness has turned into anger. Then, rather than despising ourselves, we begin patiently separating what has become confused, returning again to the root from which we came.

**Prayer**

> God of wholeness, show me where I have become divided within myself. Give me courage to see the harm I have caused without turning that seeing into shame. Untangle fear from love, craving from need, and woundedness from anger, so that I may return to what is deepest and truest in me. Lead me gently toward wholeness. Amen.
