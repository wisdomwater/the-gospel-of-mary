## Day 23 — Who Gets to Speak?

> *“Peter answered and spoke concerning these same things.”*
>
> *“He questioned them about the Savior: ‘Did he really speak with a woman in private, without our knowledge? Are we to turn around and all listen to her? Did he prefer her to us?’”*

Andrew doubts Mary's words. Peter goes further. His questions reveal that the problem is not merely the strangeness of her teaching, but Mary herself. Could the Savior really have spoken privately with her? Are the others now expected to listen? Beneath the questions we can hear another one forming: *Why her and not us?*

It is an old human tension. We like to imagine that truth will arrive through the channels we expect. We become accustomed to certain people speaking and others listening, certain voices carrying authority and others remaining at the edges. When wisdom comes from an unexpected place, we may find ourselves evaluating the messenger before we have really heard the message.

Sometimes the resistance is even more personal. Another person's gift can feel like a diminishment of our own. Their recognition can awaken our insecurity; their insight can expose something we did not see. Peter's final question is revealing: “Did he prefer her to us?” The conversation has quietly moved from truth to comparison.

Comparison makes another person's light feel like evidence of our darkness. If they are chosen, perhaps I have been overlooked. If they understand something I do not, perhaps I am less important. If they are loved, perhaps there is less love available for me. We begin competing for things that may never have been scarce.

Mary's presence invites another possibility. Someone else's gift does not have to threaten our own. We can receive wisdom from a person we did not expect without becoming smaller ourselves. We can allow another voice to carry something we need, even when that voice unsettles the arrangements with which we have become comfortable.

Perhaps spiritual maturity includes becoming teachable by unexpected people. The question shifts from *Why was this given to them?* to *What might I receive if I listen?* We do not surrender discernment, but neither do we allow status, familiarity, jealousy, or expectation to decide beforehand where truth is permitted to appear.

Grace has never been especially interested in respecting our seating arrangements.

**Prayer**

> God who speaks through unexpected voices, show me where comparison keeps me from listening. When another person's gifts awaken insecurity in me, remind me that their light does not diminish mine. Free me from deciding beforehand who can teach me, and give me the humility to recognize wisdom wherever it appears. Make my heart spacious enough to rejoice in what you give to others. Amen.
