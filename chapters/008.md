# Day 8 — When the Teacher Is Gone

> *“When he had said these things, he departed.”*
>
> *“But they were grieved. They wept greatly, saying, ‘How shall we go to the Gentiles and proclaim the good news of the kingdom of the Son of Man? If they did not spare him, how will they spare us?’”*

And then he is gone. The teaching ends without another reassurance or explanation. The one who had been answering their questions, telling them to take courage, and reminding them of the presence within them simply departs. The disciples are left standing in the silence that follows.

Their response is strikingly ordinary. They weep. Only moments earlier they were told to be courageous, yet now fear rises almost immediately. They know what happened to their teacher, and they understand what following his path might cost them. Spiritual insight has collided with the vulnerability of being human.

Perhaps we know something about this distance between what we understand and what we actually feel. We may believe that we are held by God and still wake in the night afraid. We may understand that everything changes and still grieve when something precious disappears. We may have experienced profound peace in prayer and then find ourselves overwhelmed by anxiety a few hours later. Knowing the truth does not always prevent the nervous system from trembling.

The Gospel of Mary does not shame the disciples for this. Their fear is simply allowed to appear. That may itself be a kind of grace. Courage does not require us to pretend that we are unafraid, and faith does not require us to hide our grief. Sometimes the path forward begins by admitting that the path frightens us.

There is also something important in the timing. The disciples must now discover whether the teaching can live without the physical presence of the teacher. They have been told not to look “here” or “there,” because the Son of Man is within them. Now those words must become more than an idea. The presence they had located beside them must be discovered within them.

Perhaps every spiritual path eventually reaches this threshold. What once came to us from outside must take root within. The voice we trusted becomes quieter, and we are invited to discover that what it awakened in us has not disappeared. The teacher may depart, but the teaching can become alive in those who remain.

**Prayer**

> God who remains when familiar forms disappear, stay with me in the silence after what I trusted is gone. Let me grieve without believing that grief means I have lost my way, and let me be afraid without surrendering myself to fear. Awaken within me the truth I once needed to hear from outside, until what I have received becomes something I can live. Amen.
