# Day 10 — Turning the Heart Toward the Good

> *“When Mary said these things, she turned their hearts toward the Good, and they began to discuss the words of the Savior.”*
>
> *Peter said to Mary, “Sister, we know that the Savior loved you more than the rest of women. Tell us the words of the Savior that you remember.”*

Mary does not give the disciples new information. She reminds them of what they already know, and somehow that remembrance changes the direction of the room. Moments before, their attention had been captured by danger: What will happen to us? Will they treat us as they treated him? After Mary speaks, their hearts turn toward the Good.

Perhaps much of the spiritual life is this simple movement of attention. Fear narrows our vision until the thing we are afraid of seems to occupy the entire horizon. Anger does something similar, as do shame, craving, and grief. The experience is real, but it becomes so large that we temporarily lose sight of everything else. Sometimes we do not need the difficult thing to disappear; we need our hearts gently turned until we can see more than the difficult thing.

Mary seems able to do this because she is not merely offering optimism. She points them back toward grace, toward what the Savior has awakened within them, and toward the Good that remains present even in uncertainty. Only then are they able to begin discussing his words again. Their circumstances have not changed, but the place from which they are meeting those circumstances has.

Peter then asks Mary to remember. This, too, is part of turning toward the Good. When our own vision becomes clouded, we sometimes borrow the memory of another person. One person remembers peace when another cannot. One remembers hope when another sees only loss. One remembers that love is still possible when another has become trapped in fear.

Perhaps this is one of the quiet gifts of spiritual community. We carry remembrance for one another. We do not gather because everyone is equally strong or equally certain, but because our moments of clarity rarely arrive at the same time. Today I may remind you of the Good; tomorrow I may need you to remind me.

And so Mary begins to speak. Her companions have become ready to listen because their attention has shifted from what might happen to them toward what has already been given. Sometimes revelation begins exactly there: not when every fear has been answered, but when the heart has turned enough to listen again.

**Prayer**

> Good One, when fear, anger, grief, or desire fills my vision, gently turn my heart toward you. Remind me that what troubles me is real, but it is not all that is real. Give me people who can remember grace when I forget it, and make me such a person for others. Quiet my heart until I am ready to listen again. Amen.
