# Day 22 — When Others Do Not Believe You

> *“But Andrew answered and said to the brothers, ‘Say what you think concerning what she said. For I do not believe that the Savior said this.’”*
>
> *“‘For certainly these teachings are strange ideas.’”*

Mary finishes speaking, and Andrew does not believe her. After everything she has shared—the vision, the soul's ascent, the encounters with Desire and Ignorance, the final movement into silence—his response is remarkably plain. These teachings sound strange to him, and he doubts that they came from the Savior at all.

Perhaps we expect genuine spiritual insight to carry its own undeniable authority. If an experience is deep enough, we imagine others should recognize it immediately. But human beings do not work that way. We receive one another's experiences through our own histories, expectations, beliefs, and fears. What feels luminous to one person may sound implausible to another.

Andrew's response reminds us that another person's doubt does not necessarily invalidate what we have encountered. Neither does our sincerity require another person to accept our interpretation. Mary has offered what she saw. She cannot control what happens after the words leave her mouth.

There is freedom in learning this. Much of our defensiveness comes from wanting others to confirm the reality we have experienced. When someone questions us, we may feel compelled to argue harder, explain more clearly, or persuade them that our perspective is legitimate. Sometimes those conversations are worthwhile. But there are also moments when we can simply allow another person not to understand.

This requires humility in both directions. We need not abandon our experience merely because someone doubts it, but neither must we turn our experience into unquestionable authority. What we have seen remains partial, filtered through the mystery of our own perception. We can hold it faithfully without holding it tightly.

Perhaps this is another kind of freedom from Desire: releasing the desire to be believed.

We can speak honestly about what has given us life. We can listen when others raise questions. We can remain open to correction without surrendering every insight at the first sign of disagreement. And when understanding does not come, we can entrust our experience to the same silence in which Mary's vision ended.

Not everything true within us needs to win an argument.

**Prayer**

> God of truth, give me courage to speak honestly about what I have seen and humility to remember that my seeing is never complete. When others do not understand me, free me from the need to force agreement. Help me listen without becoming defensive and remain open without abandoning what has been genuinely given. Teach me to hold truth gently, without needing to possess it or persuade. Amen.
