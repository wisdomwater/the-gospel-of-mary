## Day 14 — You Did Not See Me

> *Desire said, “I did not see you descend, but now I see you ascending. Why do you speak falsely, when you belong to me?”*
>
> *The soul answered, “I saw you. You did not see me, nor did you recognize me.”*

We return from the missing pages in the middle of a confrontation. The soul is ascending, moving toward freedom, when Desire recognizes it and makes a claim: “You belong to me.” Whatever came before has been lost, but this much remains clear. Desire assumes that because the soul once moved within its territory, the soul must still belong to it.

The soul answers with a remarkable sentence: “I saw you. You did not see me.” Desire was known, but it did not truly know the one experiencing it. Perhaps this is one of the clearest descriptions of contemplative awareness in the Gospel of Mary. Something changes when we become able to see a desire rather than simply seeing the world through it.

Usually desire speaks in the first person. *I need this. I have to have this. I cannot be content until this changes.* In the moment, there seems to be no distinction between ourselves and the wanting. But awareness introduces a little space. *There is desire here. There is craving here. There is a part of me reaching for something.* The desire remains real, but it is no longer the whole of us.

“I saw you.” These words contain no hostility. The soul does not destroy Desire or argue that it should never have existed. It simply recognizes it. And because it sees clearly, Desire's second claim—“You belong to me”—can no longer stand. What we can observe within ourselves need not become our master.

Perhaps this is why so many contemplative traditions begin with attention. Freedom often starts before behavior changes, in the quiet moment when we recognize what is happening within us. We notice anger before becoming anger, fear before letting fear narrate the future, craving before reaching automatically for satisfaction. We learn to see.

And then comes the deeper recognition: the thing we are observing does not fully know us. Desire knows what it wants from us, but it does not know the depth of who we are. Fear knows danger but not our courage. Shame knows our failures but not our belovedness. These voices may speak within us, sometimes loudly, but none of them possesses the authority to tell us who we finally are.

We can see them. They do not own us.

**Prayer**

> God of freedom, teach me to see clearly what moves within me. When desire, fear, anger, or shame says, “You belong to me,” help me create enough space to recognize its voice without becoming its voice. Let me meet what arises without hatred and without obedience. Remind me that I am deeper than what passes through me, and lead me gently into freedom. Amen.
