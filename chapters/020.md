# Day 20 — Desire Has Come to an End

> *“My desire has come to an end, and ignorance has died.”*
>
> *“In a world I was released from a world, and in a type from a heavenly type, and from the fetter of forgetfulness which is transient.”*

There is a quiet finality in the soul's words: “My desire has come to an end, and ignorance has died.” Earlier, Desire spoke as though it owned the soul. Ignorance insisted that the soul was bound. Now neither is given the final word. What once seemed powerful enough to define the soul is revealed to be temporary.

This does not necessarily mean the soul has become incapable of wanting. Desire in Mary's vision seems to mean something deeper than the ordinary movement toward food, companionship, beauty, creativity, or love. It is desire as bondage—the restless conviction that something outside us must be obtained before we can finally be whole. When that desire comes to an end, life does not become empty. Perhaps, for the first time, things can simply be received without being asked to save us.

The soul also speaks of “the fetter of forgetfulness.” This is a beautiful way of imagining our captivity. We become bound because we forget. We forget that a feeling is not an identity, that a craving is not a command, that fear is not prophecy. We forget the deeper ground from which we came, and then spend enormous energy trying to find in temporary things what we have forgotten within ourselves.

Spiritual awakening, then, may be less about acquiring something new than remembering what has always been true. The soul has been moving through Desire, Ignorance, and the powers of Wrath, but each encounter has uncovered rather than added. Layer after layer of mistaken identity falls away. What remains is not a more impressive self, but a freer one.

And Mary describes even the fetter itself as transient. This matters. Whatever binds us can feel permanent while we are inside it. A habit that has lasted years can seem inseparable from us; grief can feel endless; an old fear can seem woven into the structure of reality. Yet the soul looks upon these things and recognizes their impermanence.

The chains are temporary too.

Perhaps freedom begins with remembering this. We need not force every attachment to disappear today. We can simply stop granting it eternity. What arose can pass. What was learned can be unlearned. What has been forgotten can be remembered. And beneath the restless search for completion, we may discover that something within us has been whole all along.

**Prayer**

> God of remembrance, awaken me from the forgetfulness that makes temporary things seem ultimate. When desire tells me that I cannot be whole without what I want, help me remember the deeper ground of my life. When old bonds feel permanent, remind me that they too are passing. Let what is false gently fall away, and teach me to live from the freedom that remains. Amen.
