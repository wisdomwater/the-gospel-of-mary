# Day 28 — Go and Proclaim

> *“When Levi had said these things, they began to go out to proclaim and to preach.”*
>
> *“The Gospel according to Mary.”*

And then they go.

There is something almost startling about the simplicity of the ending. Peter does not apologize. Andrew does not announce that Mary has convinced him. We are not told that the disagreement has been settled or that everyone now understands the vision in the same way. Levi speaks, and then the community moves outward.

Perhaps we sometimes expect resolution before action. We want every question answered, every relationship repaired, every doubt settled, and every part of ourselves completely healed before we begin living what we know. But life rarely grants us such clean beginnings. We often move forward carrying unresolved questions with us.

The disciples do not seem to leave because they have finally achieved perfect agreement. They leave because something larger than their disagreement has called them. The good news entrusted to them matters more than the conflict that briefly consumed the room. Whatever tensions remain, they need not become the center around which everything else revolves.

There is wisdom here for our own inner lives as well. We can spend enormous energy waiting to feel ready. We tell ourselves that once we overcome the fear, resolve the uncertainty, understand ourselves completely, or finally become the person we think we should be, then we will begin. Yet perhaps freedom sometimes looks like moving while some uncertainty remains.

What would it mean to “proclaim” the good news in this sense? It may have little to do with convincing other people to believe something. The Gospel of Mary has repeatedly turned us toward a life becoming free from what binds it. Perhaps proclamation happens whenever that freedom becomes visible: when we refuse to make another person an adversary, when we stop allowing desire to command us, when we listen to an unexpected voice, when we choose compassion instead of judgment.

The manuscript ends, but the movement does not.

The disciples go out carrying what they have received into a world that has not suddenly become simple. Perhaps that is where every genuine spiritual encounter eventually leads. We return to ordinary life—not with every mystery solved, but changed enough to take another step.

And then another.

**Prayer**

> God who sends me back into the world, keep me from waiting until everything is resolved before I begin to live. Let what I have received become visible in the way I listen, forgive, speak, work, and love. Give me courage to move forward with unanswered questions and unfinished healing, trusting that the path itself will continue to teach me. May my life quietly proclaim the freedom I have begun to discover. Amen.
