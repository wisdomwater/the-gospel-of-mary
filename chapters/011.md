# Day 11 — I Saw the Lord

> *Mary answered and said, “What is hidden from you I will proclaim to you.”*
>
> *And she began to say these words to them: “I saw the Lord in a vision, and I said to him, ‘Lord, I saw you today in a vision.’”*

There is a quiet confidence in Mary's words. She does not claim to know everything, nor does she present herself as the keeper of every mystery. She simply offers what she has seen. Something was given to her in a vision, and because her companions have asked, she is willing to share it.

This is a different kind of knowing from the kind we usually prize. Much of our knowledge comes from gathering information, examining evidence, comparing ideas, and reasoning toward conclusions. These are valuable ways of knowing, but human experience has always included another kind: the knowledge that comes through encounter. We know friendship differently from knowing a fact about friendship. We know beauty differently from knowing its definition. We may know love long before we can adequately explain what love is.

Mary says, “I saw the Lord.” Whatever we make of the nature of her vision, the text asks us to enter the experience before explaining it. Mary encountered something that was real enough to her that it became a source of understanding. She is not arguing the others into accepting a theory. She is bearing witness to what she has experienced.

Perhaps this offers a gentle way to think about our own spiritual lives. We can become so concerned with determining what we are supposed to believe that we overlook what we have actually encountered. There may have been moments of unexpected peace, a sudden movement of compassion, a sense of presence in silence, beauty that briefly dissolved the boundary between ourselves and the world, or love that seemed larger than our ability to produce it. We need not exaggerate such moments or force them into certainty. We can simply notice them.

Mary's vision also begins in relationship. She sees, and then she speaks: “Lord, I saw you.” Contemplation is not merely observing an inner phenomenon from a distance. It is allowing ourselves to respond to what we encounter.

Perhaps prayer can begin there today—not with certainty, and not even with an explanation, but with the courage to acknowledge whatever light we have actually seen.

**Prayer**

> God of hidden things, teach me to notice what has truly been given to me. Keep me from exaggerating my experiences, but also from dismissing them because I cannot explain them. Help me recognize the moments of presence, beauty, compassion, and love that have opened something within me. Give me the humility to say only what I have seen, and the courage to receive it with gratitude. Amen.
