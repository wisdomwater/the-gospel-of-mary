# Day 27 — Put On the Perfect Human

> *“Rather, let us be ashamed and put on the perfect Human, and acquire it for ourselves as he commanded us.”*
>
> *“Let us proclaim the good news, not laying down any other rule or other law beyond what the Savior said.”*

Levi could continue the argument. He could defend Mary point by point, force Peter to answer for his words, or divide the room between those who believe her and those who do not. Instead, he redirects everyone toward something larger: “Let us put on the perfect Human.” The question is no longer who won the dispute, but what kind of people they are becoming.

The phrase is unusual, but it echoes something Mary said earlier. When the disciples were afraid, she reminded them that the Savior had prepared them and “made us truly human.” Now Levi calls them back to that vocation. Whatever spiritual transformation means in this Gospel, it is not an escape from humanity. It is a movement toward a humanity that has become less governed by fear, rivalry, craving, ignorance, and wrath.

Perhaps this gives us a useful measure for our own spiritual lives. We can accumulate beliefs, practices, insights, and experiences, but are they making us more human? Are we becoming more capable of compassion, more able to listen, less threatened by another person's gifts, more honest about our own wounds, and less dependent upon having the final word? If our spirituality makes us increasingly rigid, suspicious, or adversarial, something has become disconnected from its purpose.

Levi also returns them to the Savior's warning about making additional laws. This matters because conflict often tempts us to create new rules. We want clearer boundaries around who is right, who belongs, who may speak, and how truth must be expressed. Rules can create the feeling of safety that uncertainty denies us. Yet the community is again reminded not to turn good news into another structure of control.

The answer to their disagreement is not a tighter system. It is transformation.

Perhaps “putting on the perfect Human” is not something accomplished once. We put it on whenever we choose compassion over contempt, curiosity over certainty, courage over fear, or freedom over the need to control. Little by little, the humanity hidden beneath our defenses becomes visible.

And perhaps that is the good news they are meant to proclaim—not merely with words, but by becoming people in whom freedom has taken flesh.

**Prayer**

> God who calls me into fullness, make me more deeply human. Where fear has made me defensive, open me; where certainty has made me rigid, soften me; where comparison has made me small, enlarge my heart. Let everything I believe and practice bear fruit in compassion, courage, freedom, and love. Help me become good news before I try to speak it. Amen.
