## Day 25 — The Adversary Within

> *“Levi answered and said to Peter, ‘Peter, you have always been hot-tempered.’”*
>
> *“‘Now I see you contending against the woman like the adversaries.’”*

Levi does not enter the argument by deciding whether Mary's vision is correct. Instead, he notices what is happening to Peter. Something in Peter's manner has become adversarial, and Levi names it directly. “You have always been hot-tempered.” The conflict outside them has revealed a pattern within him.

There are moments when the content of our disagreement matters less than the spirit with which we are carrying it. We may have legitimate questions and still ask them from fear. We may defend something true while becoming unkind in the process. We may even convince ourselves that our anger is simply evidence of how deeply we care about what is right. Yet somewhere along the way, the person across from us quietly becomes an opponent.

This is especially difficult to recognize from inside ourselves. Anger narrows the field of vision. Once someone has become an adversary, we begin interpreting everything they say through that frame. Their questions sound threatening, their confidence looks arrogant, and their mistakes become evidence that our judgment of them was correct. We may still believe we are defending truth, while our deeper energy has shifted toward winning.

Levi's words invite Peter to notice that shift. Perhaps this is another expression of the inner seeing Mary described earlier. Before we can discern clearly what is happening around us, we sometimes need to recognize what is happening within us. *Why am I reacting so strongly? What am I protecting? Has this person become an enemy in my imagination?*

The goal is not to eliminate anger. Anger can alert us to injustice, violation, or something precious that needs protection. But anger is a messenger, not necessarily a trustworthy guide. If we hand it control, it can turn even a brother or sister into an adversary.

Perhaps one of the quiet signs of freedom is our ability to disagree without needing an enemy. We can question, challenge, correct, and sometimes firmly resist another person while still refusing to reduce them to an opponent. The adversary we most need to notice may not be standing across from us. It may be the adversarial spirit slowly taking shape within.

**Prayer**

> God of peace, help me notice when disagreement begins turning another person into an enemy. Let me hear what my anger is trying to tell me without allowing it to take control. Show me what I am defending, what I am afraid of, and what I may not be seeing clearly. Give me courage to speak truth without losing tenderness, and teach me to resist what is harmful without becoming adversarial in spirit. Amen.
