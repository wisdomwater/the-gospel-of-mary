# Day 13 — The Missing Pages

> *The manuscript breaks here.*
>
> *Pages 11–14 of the Gospel of Mary are missing.*

There are no words for today's reading. Four pages have disappeared from the manuscript, taking with them the beginning of Mary's revelation. One moment she is speaking with the Savior about the mind that perceives a vision; when the text returns, her vision is already underway. Whatever connected those moments has been lost.

There is something almost painful about the gap. We want to know what Mary said. We might imagine that the missing pages contained the key that would make everything else clear, and the temptation is to reconstruct them from hints in what survives. But the honest response is simpler: we do not know. The silence in the manuscript cannot be made to speak.

Much of life contains pages like these. There are questions for which we never receive satisfying answers, relationships whose endings remain unresolved, losses that leave us wondering what might have been, and parts of our own stories whose meaning never becomes entirely clear. We often respond by filling the gaps. The mind constructs explanations because uncertainty feels harder to bear than almost any answer.

Contemplation teaches another possibility. We can allow a blank space to remain blank. We can sit before what we do not know without treating uncertainty as a problem that must immediately be solved. This is not indifference or intellectual laziness. It is humility—the recognition that reality is larger than what has been preserved for us, larger than what we can reconstruct, and larger than what we can understand.

Perhaps there is even something sacred about the missing pages. They remind us that we are receiving a fragment rather than possessing the whole. Mary's voice reaches us across centuries through damaged papyrus, incomplete memories, translation, and time. Yet what remains can still awaken something within us.

We do not need every page in order to listen.

And perhaps we do not need every answer in order to live faithfully either. Sometimes the invitation is simply to stand at the edge of what is known, resist the urge to fill the silence, and trust that mystery does not have to be solved before it can be entered.

**Prayer**

> God of what is known and what remains hidden, teach me to live gently with unanswered questions. When I am tempted to fill every silence with certainty, give me the humility to say, “I do not know.” Help me trust that what is missing need not make what remains meaningless. Meet me in the blank spaces, and teach me to listen even there. Amen.
