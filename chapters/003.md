## Day 3 — Returning to Our Nature

> *“That is why the Good came among you, to the essence of every nature, in order to restore it to its root.”*
>
> *Then he continued and said, “That is why you become sick and die, for you love what deceives you.”*

If yesterday’s words unsettled our usual understanding of sin, today’s words begin to show us what salvation might mean in the Gospel of Mary. The Good comes not merely to declare us forgiven, but to restore us. Something in us has become confused about where we belong, and the work of the Good is to lead us back toward our root, toward the deepest truth of what we are.

There is tenderness in that image. Restoration assumes that beneath everything tangled and wounded, something remains worth restoring. A painting may become covered with grime, a garden overgrown with weeds, or a familiar path obscured by years of neglect, yet none of these things has ceased to be what it is. The work is not to destroy them and begin again, but to uncover what has been there all along.

The next words are more difficult. We suffer because we “love what deceives” us. Perhaps deception here is not simply believing something false, but mistaking something temporary for something capable of carrying the full weight of our longing. We ask possessions to make us secure, achievements to tell us we matter, approval to assure us that we are lovable, and pleasure to quiet an ache that soon returns. None of these things is necessarily evil. The deception begins when we ask them to become our root.

And so restoration may involve learning where to place our weight. We can enjoy what is temporary without expecting it to make us permanent. We can receive affection without requiring another person to establish our worth. We can accomplish meaningful things without asking accomplishment to tell us who we are. Slowly, the center of gravity moves away from everything that must eventually change and toward something deeper.

Perhaps this is what the Good is doing among us even now. Not dragging us somewhere foreign, but returning us to ourselves. Beneath the many things we have mistaken for home, there remains a quieter place from which we came and in which we already belong. The invitation is not to become someone else, but to release what deceives us long enough to remember our root.

**Prayer**

> Good One, lead me beneath the things I have mistaken for home. Show me gently where I have asked temporary things to satisfy an eternal longing. Restore what has become confused within me, and teach me to rest my life upon what is deepest and most enduring. Bring me home to the root of who I am. Amen.
