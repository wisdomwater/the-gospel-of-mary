# Day 26 — Who Are You to Reject Her?

> *“But if the Savior made her worthy, who are you indeed to reject her?”*
>
> *“Surely the Savior knows her very well. That is why he loved her more than us.”*

Levi asks a question that reaches beneath the argument: “Who are you to reject her?” Peter has been evaluating Mary as though her place among them depends upon his approval. Levi turns the whole arrangement upside down. Mary does not need Peter to establish her worth. The Savior already knows her.

There is something deeply freeing in the thought of being known before we are evaluated. Much of life teaches us to establish our worth through performance. We demonstrate competence, usefulness, intelligence, goodness, attractiveness, or spiritual maturity and then wait, often without realizing it, for someone else to confirm that we belong. When approval comes, we feel secure for a while. When it is withheld, the ground beneath us can begin to shake.

Levi points Mary toward another ground entirely. “The Savior knows her very well.” Her worth does not begin with the disciples' agreement about her. She has been seen and known in a place deeper than their judgments can reach. Peter may accept her or reject her, understand her or misunderstand her, but he cannot create or erase the reality of her belovedness.

Perhaps we need this reminder because other people's opinions can become disproportionately powerful. A criticism can linger in the mind long after a dozen words of encouragement have faded. Rejection can make us question things we once knew clearly. We begin looking outward for someone to return to us the worth we imagine they have taken away.

But what if the deepest part of us cannot be granted or revoked by another person? What if being known by God comes before being understood by anyone else? Then approval can still feel good and rejection can still hurt, but neither has to become the foundation of our identity.

There is also an invitation here in how we see others. If another person is already deeply known by God, perhaps we can hold our judgments more humbly. We may disagree, establish boundaries, or recognize harm without imagining that we possess the final word about who someone is.

The soul in Mary's vision has been shedding false identities all along. Perhaps this is another one to release: the self whose worth depends upon being accepted by everyone in the room.

**Prayer**

> God who knows me completely, root my life more deeply in being known by you. When approval makes me feel worthy or rejection makes me question my worth, remind me that neither can define the deepest truth of who I am. Help me extend that same grace to others, remembering that I never see the whole of another person. Let me live from belovedness rather than from the endless need to prove that I belong. Amen.
