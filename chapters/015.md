## Day 15 — The Garment I Wore

> *“I served you as a garment, and you did not know me.”*
>
> *“When it had said this, it departed rejoicing greatly.”*

The soul's encounter with Desire ends with an unusual image: a garment. Desire had wrapped itself around the soul so closely that the two might have appeared to be one thing. Yet the soul now understands that what covered it was never identical with what it was. A garment can be worn for a very long time and still be removed.

We accumulate many such garments over the course of a life. Some are given to us by others: expectations about who we should become, judgments we learned to accept as truth, roles we were rewarded for playing. Others grow from within us. We learn ways of protecting ourselves, seeking approval, avoiding pain, satisfying desire, or controlling uncertainty. Eventually these patterns become so familiar that we stop experiencing them as things we do. They begin to feel like who we are.

But familiarity is not identity. A person who has spent years pleasing others is more than the need for approval. A person who repeatedly reaches for comfort is more than the craving for comfort. A person who has learned to protect themselves with anger is more than their anger. Even patterns that have accompanied us for decades may be garments rather than skin.

This does not mean we can simply decide to remove every pattern that constrains us. Some garments have been worn so long that taking them off requires patience, compassion, practice, and sometimes the help of others. Yet awareness changes the relationship. Once we recognize, “This is something I learned to wear,” another possibility appears. What has been learned can be examined. What has become automatic can slowly become conscious.

Then comes a beautiful detail: the soul departs “rejoicing greatly.” Freedom is not described primarily as a grim battle against Desire. The soul sees clearly, recognizes what no longer defines it, and moves onward with joy.

Perhaps transformation can sometimes feel like this. We do not have to hate the garments that once covered us. Some may even have helped us survive a season of life. We can thank them for what they provided and still discover that they no longer fit. Beneath them remains something quieter and freer—the self that wore them, but was never contained by them.

**Prayer**

> God who knows me beneath every covering, help me recognize the garments I have mistaken for myself. Give me compassion for the patterns that once protected or comforted me, and courage to release what no longer leads toward life. Remind me that I am deeper than my habits, roles, fears, and cravings. Lead me forward lightly, and let freedom become joy. Amen.
