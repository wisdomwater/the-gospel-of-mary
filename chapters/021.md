## Day 21 — Rest in Silence

> *“From this time on I attain to the rest of the time, of the season, of the aeon, in silence.”*
>
> *After she had said these things, Mary became silent, since it was to this point that the Savior had spoken with her.*

Mary's vision ends not with triumph, but with silence. The soul has passed through the powers that claimed it, accused it, and attempted to define it. Desire has ended, ignorance has died, forgetfulness has loosened its grip. And beyond all of this striving lies rest.

There is something fitting about Mary becoming silent immediately after describing it. She does not explain the silence or offer a theory about what happens there. The form of the story begins to embody its meaning. Words have carried us as far as they can, and then they stop.

We spend so much of life trying to arrive somewhere. Even spiritual practice can become another project of self-improvement: more peaceful, more awakened, more disciplined, more loving. We measure ourselves against an imagined future self and turn transformation into another form of striving. Yet Mary's soul ultimately arrives somewhere that cannot be achieved in the ordinary sense. It rests.

Perhaps this rest is not inactivity but freedom from the constant need to become. For a moment, nothing has to be fixed, acquired, defended, explained, or overcome. Desire no longer pulls us toward what is missing. Ignorance no longer tells us who we are. The voices that demanded our attention have grown quiet, and we discover that beneath them there was always a deeper stillness.

Contemplative prayer sometimes gives us a glimpse of this. We sit without trying to manufacture an experience. Thoughts arise and pass. Desires appear and disappear. We notice the mind reaching for something to solve, remember, rehearse, or understand, and gently release it. We are not trying to make silence happen. We are discovering the silence that remains when we stop filling every moment.

Mary knows when to stop speaking. Perhaps there is wisdom in knowing when we should stop as well. Not every mystery becomes clearer through more words. Not every prayer needs another request. Sometimes the most faithful response to what we have seen is simply to become quiet.

And so today, rather than trying to understand Mary's final words, perhaps we can let them lead us where they led her.

Into rest.

Into silence.

**Prayer**

> God of the silence beneath all things, quiet in me what is always striving to arrive somewhere else. For these few moments, let there be nothing I need to accomplish, understand, or become. As thoughts and desires arise, help me release them gently and return to your presence. Teach me to rest in the silence where nothing is missing. Amen.
