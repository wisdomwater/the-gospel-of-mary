## Day 7 — Beyond Another Law

> *“Go then and proclaim the good news of the kingdom.”*
>
> *“Do not lay down any rule beyond what I appointed for you, nor give a law like the lawgiver, lest you be constrained by it.”*

There is an irony that seems to follow nearly every spiritual awakening. Someone discovers a way into greater freedom, and before long we begin constructing rules about how everyone else must get there. An experience becomes a teaching, the teaching becomes a system, and eventually the system can become another burden. What began as a path toward liberation slowly hardens into something we must defend and obey.

The Savior’s warning in the Gospel of Mary is striking because it comes immediately before he leaves the disciples. They are being entrusted with something precious, yet they are warned not to turn that gift into another law. They are to proclaim good news, but they are not to become new lawgivers. Perhaps there is wisdom here about the temptation to confuse the path that helped us with the path everyone must follow.

Rules can be useful. Practices, traditions, disciplines, and communities can give shape to our lives and help us remember what matters. But even good practices can become constraining when we forget what they were meant to serve. Prayer can become an obligation we measure ourselves against. Spiritual disciplines can become evidence of our worthiness. A teaching intended to open the heart can become another standard by which we judge ourselves and others.

The deeper question may be whether our spiritual life is making us more free to love. If a practice makes us more present, compassionate, patient, and awake, it is serving its purpose. If it makes us increasingly anxious about getting everything right, perhaps the practice has quietly become a lawgiver. We may have begun serving the form rather than allowing the form to serve transformation.

The Gospel of Mary gives us permission to hold even our spiritual structures lightly. We can receive wisdom without turning it into a cage. We can walk a path faithfully without insisting that every person walk exactly as we do. The good news remains good precisely because it leads toward freedom, toward a life increasingly rooted in love rather than fear. Perhaps the best traditions are those that teach us how to walk until, almost without noticing, we discover that we are walking freely.

**Prayer**

> God of freedom, thank you for the practices and teachings that have helped me find my way. Keep me from turning them into burdens for myself or others. Teach me to recognize when a helpful form has become a rigid law, and lead me back to the freedom for which it was given. May everything I practice make me more present, more compassionate, and more able to love. Amen.
