## Day 1 — Returning to the Root

> *“Will matter then be destroyed, or not?”*
>
> *The Savior said, “All natures, all formations, all creatures exist in and with one another, and they will be resolved again into their own roots.”*

We enter the Gospel of Mary late. The first six pages are missing, and whatever introduction once carried us into this conversation has disappeared with them. We arrive instead as though we have quietly opened a door and found Jesus already speaking with his disciples, just in time to hear someone ask what must have seemed like an enormous question: What happens to all of this? Does matter itself eventually disappear?

The answer attributed to Jesus does not describe destruction so much as return. Everything that has taken form has arisen from something deeper, and everything will eventually be “resolved again into its own roots.” The image is almost organic. A leaf falls and becomes soil; a body returns to the earth; the things we build eventually lose their shape and become material for something else. Forms appear, remain for a while, and pass away, while the mystery from which they came continues beneath them.

Much of our suffering comes from asking temporary things to remain permanent. We want bodies not to age, relationships not to change, achievements not to fade, and seasons of happiness not to end. Even the identities we construct for ourselves can become things we desperately protect. Yet Mary begins by placing us in a world where forms are not failures because they pass away. Their impermanence belongs to their nature.

There can be something strangely comforting in this. What passes away does not necessarily fall into nothingness. It returns to its root. The Gospel does not explain precisely what that root is, and perhaps we do not need to rush to explain it either. For today, it may be enough to notice that beneath all our grasping at forms is a deeper invitation to trust the ground from which they arise.

Perhaps the spiritual life begins not by learning how to hold everything together, but by loosening our grip. We can love what has been given without demanding that it remain unchanged. We can receive this particular day, this particular body, these particular people, knowing that every form is temporary and therefore precious. And somewhere beneath all that changes, we can begin listening for the root.

**Prayer**

> Source of all that is, help me receive what takes form without trying to possess it forever. When I am frightened by change, teach me to trust the deeper ground from which life comes and to which life returns. Let me hold this day gently, grateful for what has been given, and willing to release what cannot remain. Amen.
