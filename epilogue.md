## Epilogue

You have reached the end of the Gospel of Mary, though perhaps “the end” is not quite the right way to describe it.

The manuscript ends with movement. The disciples do not remain gathered around Mary discussing her vision forever. They go out. They carry what they have received back into a world where fear, desire, conflict, misunderstanding, and uncertainty have not disappeared. The gospel ends, but the practice of becoming free continues.

Perhaps that is where these readings should leave us as well.

We have followed a soul through voices that tried to claim it. Desire said, *You belong to me.* Ignorance said, *You are bound.* Other powers accused, questioned, and attempted to define it. Yet the soul slowly discovered something deeper than all of them. It could see what moved within it without becoming what it saw.

May you discover that same spaciousness.

When desire arises, may you recognize it without hatred and without surrendering your freedom to it. When fear speaks, may you hear what it is trying to protect without allowing it to narrate the whole future. When anger comes, may you listen carefully enough to distinguish what is true within it from the false wisdom it sometimes carries.

When old names return—failure, unworthy, unwanted, not enough—may you remember that no passing voice possesses the authority to tell you who you finally are.

May you become attentive to the quieter presence beneath them all.

May you learn to trust the inner eye that sees without grasping. May you become comfortable with unanswered questions and missing pages. May you resist the temptation to turn every insight into a rule, every experience into a certainty, or every disagreement into a contest that must be won.

May you become more fully human.

May your spirituality make you softer without making you weak, courageous without making you hard, discerning without making you suspicious, and free without making you indifferent to the needs of others. May whatever you discover within yourself become visible in the way you listen, forgive, work, speak, and love.

And when you reach the places where words can carry you no farther, may you know when to become silent.

There is nothing more you need to extract from these fragments. Mary herself eventually stopped speaking.

So close the book gently.

Return to the life waiting for you.

Carry what has become alive within you into the ordinary places of your days, and when something asks again who you are, remember the soul that passed through every power that tried to possess it.

See clearly.

Walk freely.

And when you can, rest in silence.

Amen.
