# The Gospel of Mary

### A Modern Devotional

The *Gospel of Mary* survives only in fragments.

Its opening six pages are missing. Four more disappear just as Mary begins recounting a vision of the risen Christ. What remains is brief, mysterious, and remarkably beautiful—a gospel concerned with inner freedom, spiritual perception, desire, fear, human wholeness, and the journey of the soul toward rest.

This project is a **28-day modern devotional reading of the surviving Gospel of Mary**.

Rather than attempting to reconstruct the portions of the ancient text that have been lost, these readings stay close to what remains. Each day begins with a small fragment of the Gospel, followed by a contemplative reflection and a short guided prayer.

## About the Gospel

The principal surviving manuscript of the *Gospel of Mary* is found in the **Berlin Codex (Papyrus Berolinensis 8502)**, a Coptic manuscript generally dated to the fifth century.

The surviving text begins on manuscript page 7. Pages 1–6 are lost, as are pages 11–14. Two earlier Greek fragments of the Gospel also survive, demonstrating that the work was circulating in Greek centuries before the surviving Coptic manuscript was copied, but neither fragment restores the missing sections.

The Gospel begins for us in the middle of a conversation between the Savior and his disciples. After the Savior departs, the disciples become frightened and Mary encourages them, turning their hearts toward the Good.

At Peter's request, she then recounts a revelation she received from the Savior. Her vision describes the soul encountering powers including Desire, Ignorance, and Wrath as it moves toward freedom and ultimately toward rest in silence.

Mary's authority is then challenged by Andrew and Peter. Levi comes to her defense, reminding the others that if the Savior considered her worthy, they should not reject her.

The Gospel ends simply: the disciples go out to proclaim the good news.

## The Devotional

The book contains **28 daily readings**, following the surviving Gospel in sequence.

Each reading uses the same simple structure:

**Day X — Title**

*A short passage from the Gospel of Mary*

A contemplative reflection of approximately 400 words, allowing the ancient text to open into questions of ordinary human experience.

**Prayer**

A brief guided prayer inviting the reader to move from reflection into presence.

The missing pages themselves are not ignored. One reading pauses deliberately at the four-page gap in Mary's revelation, allowing the silence of the manuscript to become part of the contemplative journey.

## Major Themes

Throughout the readings, several themes return:

* returning to our root
* becoming truly human
* attachment and suffering
* desire without bondage
* recognizing that our inner movements are not our identity
* the Son of Man within
* *nous* and inner perception
* freedom from fear, ignorance, and wrath
* spiritual authority and unexpected voices
* resisting the temptation to turn liberation into another law
* learning to live with uncertainty and missing pages
* rest and silence

At the heart of the book is a simple possibility: **what moves within us does not have to possess us**.

Desire can be seen without being obeyed. Fear can be felt without becoming prophecy. Anger can be heard without being mistaken for wisdom. Our wounds, habits, failures, and roles may be real without containing the whole truth of who we are.

## Approach

This is not intended as a critical edition, scholarly commentary, or reconstruction of the original Gospel.

It is a **modern contemplative reading**.

Where the ancient text is uncertain or incomplete, the devotional does not attempt to manufacture certainty. The goal is to let the surviving words speak first and then explore how their images might illuminate the inner life of a contemporary reader.

The distinction between ancient text and modern reflection is intentional. Mary's words provide the starting point; the devotional sections are invitations to contemplation rather than claims about the only possible interpretation of the Gospel.

## A Gospel in Fragments

There is something fitting about receiving Mary's Gospel this way.

Most of us do not receive our own lives as complete manuscripts either. We understand them in pieces. Some chapters become clear only years later. Others seem to contain missing pages of their own.

The *Gospel of Mary* comes to us damaged, incomplete, and nevertheless alive.

This devotional is an invitation to sit with what remains.

To see clearly.

To walk freely.

And, when words have carried us as far as they can, to rest in silence.
